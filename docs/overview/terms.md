## Content policy

The Service does not host content uploaded by users. Each instance's document library is ingested by its operator from named publishers, and the AI features only summarise, tag and search what is in that library. Even so, we apply an explicit content policy to everything the Service stores, displays or distributes, including AI-generated summaries and tags.

**Prohibited content.** The following must not be present in the library or in any output of the Service:

- Content that is illegal to publish or distribute in the operator's jurisdiction, including child sexual abuse material, terrorist content, and content whose distribution is prohibited by court order.
- Personal data of private individuals that was not lawfully published, such as contact details, identification numbers, health or financial records.
- Material that infringes copyright, licensing terms or other rights of third parties.
- Content that incites violence or hatred against people on the basis of a protected characteristic.
- Malicious code, or content used to deceive users into disclosing credentials or making payments.

**Reporting.** Anyone can report content through either route:

- **In the application:** every search result, document summary, AI summary, tag and chat answer has a rating control. Open it, give the item a one-star rating and state in the comment that you are reporting it and why (for example "illegal content", "personal data", "copyright"). Ratings capture the surrounding context so reviewers see exactly what was shown. Visitors without an account can report through the floating **Feedback** button, again with the reason in the comment.
- **By email:** {{CONTACT_EMAIL_LINK}}. Include a link to the item and the reason.

**Moderation.** Ratings and feedback go to an administrator queue where each one is triaged (open, acknowledged, information needed, resolved, won't fix) with reviewer notes, so a report is never lost among quality ratings: administrators filter the queue by low score and search comments for report keywords. An administrator can hide a document from the whole platform in one action, which removes it from search, listings, the research assistant and the AI protocol endpoints while keeping an audit record, and can restore it if the report is not upheld. Content that breaches this policy is hidden promptly on confirmation and, where appropriate, removed from the source library. We do not notify the reporter individually unless we need more information.

**Self-hosted instances.** Operators of their own instance are responsible for the content they ingest and for handling reports about it. The reporting controls, administrator queue and hide action ship with the software so every deployment has the same mechanism.
